## Voice and system rules (summary)
- **Curiosity rule.** Every page earns the next tap with a hook: curiosity, tension, recognition, contradiction, humour, a sting, a reveal. Never resolve a hook on the page that creates it. If a page could appear in another dating app, it doesn't belong here.
- **Hard language rule.** Nothing that sounds like therapy, coaching, self-help, wellness, HR, or an assessment. The voice is the smart, funny friend who says "okay, you know what's funny about you?" Humour from recognition, never at the user's expense.
- **Identity rule.** Nothing is presented as a permanent identity. These are patterns currently running. Consumer-facing term: **"Your Autopilot, the patterns running the show."** Never "archetype" in the UI (the underlying archetype logic is unchanged). **Blind Spots** replaces "awkward moments" as a category.
- **Buttons** are opportunities for voice, never generic ("Continue", "Next", "Get Started").
- **Payment boundary.** No disguised questions, photo uploads, or personal disclosure before the price is shown and accepted.
- **Reinforcement.** Intermittent, variable reward: Felix appears at specific question counts (above), rewarding the user by implying he already knows something.

## Visual direction (from the client's follow-up)
Extract the DNA of places like the Lafayette (colour, pattern, texture, typography, layering, wit, discovery) and build an original digital world from it. **Not** hotel rooms, couches, lamps, lobbies, or lifestyle photography; those are references, not content.
- Colour: unexpected, saturated combinations; not black-on-black.
- Pattern: stripes, geometrics, ornament, repetition as graphic surfaces, borders, transitions.
- Texture: lacquer, velvet, tile, aged paper, metal, signage, translated digitally through depth, grain, layering, finish.
- Type: the confidence of iconic old signage, not a font that says "vintage".
- Composition: maximalist detail with enough negative space to feel expensive.
- Surprise: something changes every few screens (pattern, scale, colour, type, motion).
- Energy: social, cheeky, curious, sophisticated, slightly irreverent. Not cozy, not nightclub, not hotel.
- The interface should behave like Felix: evidence before cleverness, intermittent reinforcement, ouch then laugh then oh-shit, surprise without randomness.
- Test: strip every photograph; would type, colour, pattern, texture, buttons and transitions alone hold someone for 30 minutes?
- Their own note on the mockups: "minus WAY too many quails". The quail is a wink, used sparingly.
